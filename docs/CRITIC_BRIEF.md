## Largest concrete gaps

1. **P1 — MentorOS removes its decision summary on phone.** The desktop top summary visibly says “Review 3 suggested changes” and “Your approval needed.” At 390 × 844, only “7 commitments” and “3 hours” remain before the two portraits; the pending summary is absent. “Proposed plan 3” is visible near the bottom of the first screen, but it does not explain the required human action. The approve button was approximately 2,554 CSS pixels below the initial viewport top. Preserve the pending summary on phone and make it a direct jump to the review panel. Acceptance: the initial phone screen explicitly identifies the pending decision and offers a route to it.

2. **P1 — MentorOS makes users traverse a full screen of duplicated rationale before approval.** The phone screenshot of the proposal contains the joint summary, then long Mira and Atlas paragraphs; the approval controls are still below that screen. Those paragraphs recur in the adjacent/later conversation. Atlas also visibly says “deterministic validation's maximumStudyMinutes,” and the heading talks about validation rather than simply stating the decision. Put the three exact time changes and the approval controls together, with detailed coach reasoning in an expandable section. Acceptance: the user can compare the changed times and see the approval action in one review area without traversing the duplicated transcript.

3. **P2 — DealGuard's detailed decision is ordered after the supplier composer on phone.** After one phone page scroll, the screenshot showed comparison totals, a long conversation, and the “New supplier message” form; “Buyer recommendation” was still roughly 1,023 CSS pixels below that viewport top. The first-screen hold banner does communicate the immediate decision, which is why the observed flow passes, but its policy limit, cited evidence, and exact next step require considerable further scrolling. Place the buyer recommendation before the conversation at narrow widths, or add a visible jump from the hold banner.

4. **P2 — MentorOS's source drawer exposes a raw extraction rather than a concise reason to trust the recommendation.** The Open Group source card repeats its long title, includes literal `#` markers and navigation phrases such as “Availability Read the HTML Edition Online Login to Download,” and ends in a truncated word in the accessibility text. The desktop screenshot shows that excerpt filling the drawer while the actual source link lies below the visible content. Show the publication title/domain, one relevant excerpt, the connection to the study block, and the link together. Its footer's “opening hours can change” is also irrelevant to this study publication.
